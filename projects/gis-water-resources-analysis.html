Project overview

This work brings together GIS data preparation and spatial analysis to support water-resources and watershed-related engineering tasks.

Detailed working methodology

01

Define the GIS objective and study area

Clarify the engineering question and identify the boundary and spatial layers needed to answer it. Record each dataset's source, date, scale or resolution, and intended use.

Keep the coordinate reference system and units consistent with the intended measurements and map output.

02

Inspect and prepare source data

Review vector and raster layers for missing data, invalid geometry, unexpected attribute values and coordinate-system mismatches.

Reproject or convert data only when needed, preserving original source files and recording processing steps so the workflow can be repeated.

03

Clip and organize spatial layers

Use the study boundary to extract the relevant portions of source layers. Organize the outputs with meaningful names and keep a clear distinction between source data and derived data.

Check that clipped layers cover the intended study area and align correctly when displayed together.

04

Process terrain and thematic information

Where required by the study, prepare terrain-derived layers such as slope or elevation and thematic layers such as land cover or soil.

Check raster resolution, NoData areas, classification fields and cell alignment before combining layers. The exact processing sequence depends on the data and research objective.

05

Perform spatial analysis

Apply suitable GIS operations such as overlay, reclassification, spatial selection, attribute calculation or summary statistics according to the stated objective.

Verify field names, data types, units and output records after processing. Avoid presenting a derived layer as a verified result until its attributes and spatial alignment have been checked.

06

Prepare the map layout

Build a readable map with a clear title, legend, scale bar, north arrow where appropriate, coordinate reference information and data-source notes.

Use a visual hierarchy that makes the main result easy to interpret without hiding uncertainty or the limits of the input data.

07

Quality-check and export

Inspect the final map at its intended display or print size. Confirm that labels, symbology, scale, extent and attribution are legible.

Export to an appropriate format and resolution, retain the project files for reproducibility, and publish only layers and maps that you are authorized to share.
